Australia · late 1980s onward

Melbourne Shuffle

Fast heel-toe footwork done to hard dance music, named after the city whose warehouse scene produced it.

The shuffle started in Melbourne's underground clubs in the late 1980s and hardened through the 1990s alongside local hard house and hard trance. The core is the running man — a stationary jog where the trailing foot slides back as the leading knee comes up — combined with the T-step, a sideways heel-and-toe pivot.

It is a solo dance done in a crowd, judged on speed, cleanliness and how little the upper body wobbles. Dedicated shufflers taped the soles of their shoes or sprinkled talc on the floor to get a cleaner slide.

Export

A wave of camcorder and then phone footage pushed it out of Australia in the mid-2000s. It fused with hardstyle in the Netherlands, picked up a huge following in Malaysia, and eventually looped back into mainstream pop videos with most of the local history filed off.

Core moves

Running man

Jog in place while the back foot slides. The engine of the whole style.

T-step

Sideways travel on alternating heel and toe pivots.

Spin

A full turn dropped into the run without losing the count.
